What is this? How do I use it?

The story: I've made beats on everything — open-source tools on Ubuntu Studio, an MPC2000XL in my hardware-only years (tracked out into Pro Tools), and FL Studio today. Beat Lab is my own take: FL's hands, the MPC's brain, a Pro Tools finish — in a web page. Start a beat on one machine, finish it on the other.

How it works: Open it in Chrome or Edge. Point the sound browser at your sample folder — on Windows that's the Google Drive folder, on the MacBook it's the external drive — click sounds to preview them, load them into channels, and build. Everything saves as one small project file; the sounds stay in your library.

Feedback? Catch me live — I test it on the Tuesday Cook-Up, Tuesdays 8–10 PM ET on YouTube.

Inside the lab

  • Your sounds, your library — preview before you load, FL browser style, with hot swapping: change the kick mid-loop and the beat never stops
  • Sequences & song mode — MPC-style sequences strung into a playlist; duplicate a sequence for variations
  • Waves up top — drop in a sound effect or a DJ drop, see its wave, shift it off-grid against the sequencing; even snap a kick in as a wave when you want timing the grid can't give you. Convert any sequence to a wave and keep working it as audio
  • Piano roll with your own sounds — pitched samples, 808s, and chops; chord and progression generators; a key finder that follows your flip and tunes the 808 to it
  • An MPK brain — full MIDI mapping with a ready map for the MPK mini mk3; record all pad channels at once, Add or Overwrite, quantized or raw, then quantize after the fact with strength
  • Real mixer — per-sound gain plus channel faders with VU meters, your names, your colors
  • The finish — aligned WAV stems per channel and a 320 kbps master MP3, rendered from the whole song chain
